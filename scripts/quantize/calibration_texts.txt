Squaring both sides gives 2 = p^2 / q^2, and rearranging yields p^2 = 2 * q^2. This means p^2 is even, since it equals two times an integer. This contradiction shows that our original assumption must be false, and therefore sqrt(2) cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers. Hence sqrt(2) is irrational, a result first proven by the ancient Greeks and considered one of the earliest examples of a proof by contradiction in mathematics.

The concept of opportunity cost, one of the foundational ideas in economics, refers to the value of the next best alternative that must be given up in order to pursue a certain action. Consider a student deciding whether to spend a Saturday afternoon studying for an upcoming exam or working a part-time shift that pays fifteen dollars an hour. The opportunity cost of studying is not simply zero, even though no money changes hands directly; it is the fifteen dollars in wages the student forgoes by choosing not to work that afternoon.
